# Product Growth Lab — Retention & Cohort Analysis

## Key Findings

### 1. Repeat Engagement Declines Sharply

Analysis of Retailrocket's e-commerce activity data found exact-day retention rates of **2.77% on D1, 0.56% on D7, and 0.16% on D30**.

These results indicate that most visitors do not return on the measured days following their first observed interaction.

### 2. Retention Varies Across Acquisition Cohorts

Weekly cohort analysis revealed differences in returning behavior. The earliest displayed cohort recorded 5.15% D1 retention and 1.39% D7 retention, while many subsequent cohorts recorded lower rates.

These differences warrant further investigation into visitor composition, purchasing intent, and behavioral patterns. Partial cohort observation and differences in eligible cohort sizes must be considered.

### 3. Early Activation Is Associated With Higher Retention

Visitors who added an item to their cart on their first observed day showed higher subsequent retention than visitors who did not activate:

| Metric | Activated | Not Activated |
|---|---:|---:|
| D1 | 8.91% | 2.65% |
| D7 | 1.71% | 0.54% |
| D30 | 0.33% | 0.16% |

Day 0 activation was associated with approximately 3.17 times higher D7 retention.

This is an observational relationship and does not establish causation.

## Product Implications

The combination of low cart activation and low returning activity suggests that improving early product engagement is a reasonable area for further investigation.

The next analytical step is to segment visitors by browsing intensity, repeat activity, cart behavior, and purchasing outcomes to identify which groups offer the most promising opportunities for product improvements.

## Limitations

The dataset records anonymous e-commerce interactions rather than verified new registrations. Retention is defined using exact calendar-day activity, eligible populations differ by observation window, and the analysis does not measure subscription churn.
